# Findings: U.S. CPI and Inflation Trends

## Project Overview

This project examines monthly changes in the U.S. Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U) from January 1982 through the latest available observation included in the workbook. Using Microsoft Excel, I analyzed long-term changes in the CPI, calculated monthly inflation rates, and explored periods of greater price volatility.

## Key Findings

### 1. The overall price level increased substantially over time

The CPI rose from 94.3 in January 1982 to approximately 335.0 in August 2026. This represents an increase of approximately 255% in the index over the period.

This long-term trend indicates that the overall consumer price level increased substantially. Because the CPI measures the price of a representative basket of consumer goods and services, the increase also illustrates why the same amount of money generally purchases less over time when income does not keep pace with rising prices.

### 2. Monthly inflation rates fluctuated throughout the period

Month-to-month changes in the CPI were not constant. The monthly inflation rate varied between positive and negative values, reflecting periods when the index increased or decreased.

Across the observations with calculable monthly rates, the average monthly change was approximately 0.24%. This is an average of monthly percentage changes, not an annual inflation rate.

Monthly changes provide a useful view of short-term price movements, but they should not be confused with year-over-year inflation, which compares the CPI with the same month in the previous year.

### 3. Some periods experienced more pronounced monthly changes

The largest positive monthly inflation rate in the calculated observations occurred in June 2022, at approximately 1.37%. The largest negative monthly rate occurred in November 2008, at approximately -1.92%.

These observations identify months with unusually large movements in the CPI. They warrant further investigation in the context of economic conditions during those periods. However, a large monthly movement alone does not establish its cause.

### 4. Recent years show a higher CPI index than earlier decades

The average monthly CPI index was approximately 259 in 2020, 271 in 2021, and 293 in 2022. It continued to rise in 2023 and 2024.

This pattern shows that the overall price level continued increasing during these years, even as the pace of monthly changes varied. A declining inflation rate would not necessarily mean prices were falling; it could simply mean that prices were rising more slowly.

### 5. Missing observations require careful handling

The workbook contains missing observations, and some months do not have enough information to calculate a monthly inflation rate.

Missing values were not treated as zero. A zero inflation rate represents an actual month in which the CPI did not change, while a blank indicates that the rate could not be calculated from the available data.

This distinction is important because replacing missing observations with zero could distort the analysis and produce misleading conclusions.

## Economic Interpretation

The CPI provides a measure of changes in the general price level, which is closely related to the purchasing power of money. When the CPI increases, a given amount of money generally buys less of the representative basket of goods and services.

However, CPI alone cannot determine whether a particular household is better or worse off financially. To evaluate changes in household purchasing power, CPI trends would need to be compared with income or wage growth over the same period.

## Limitations

* Monthly inflation rates can be volatile and do not provide the same information as year-over-year inflation.
* CPI represents an average basket of consumer goods and services and may not reflect the spending patterns of every household.
* Missing observations limit the months for which monthly inflation rates can be calculated.
* The analysis describes patterns in the data but does not establish the causes of individual CPI movements.
* The latest year in the dataset is incomplete, so its results should not be directly compared with full calendar years without accounting for the difference in coverage.

## Conclusion

This analysis demonstrates how Excel can be used to explore long-term changes in consumer prices and short-term fluctuations in inflation. The results show a substantial increase in the CPI over the study period, alongside considerable month-to-month variation.

The project also highlights the importance of distinguishing the price level from the inflation rate, handling missing observations appropriately, and recognizing the limitations of descriptive analysis when interpreting economic data.
